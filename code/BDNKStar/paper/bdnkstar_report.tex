\title{BDNKStar: neutron-star oscillation modes in causal first-order viscous hydrodynamics ---
methods, mode census, frame robustness, and a 3+1D engine validation}

\author{N. Fernandes}
\affiliation{California Institute of Technology, Pasadena, CA 91125, USA}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We document the numerical methods implemented in \textsc{BDNKStar}, a Julia toolkit for
relativistic stellar oscillations with Bemfica--Disconzi--Noronha--Kovtun (\BDNK) causal
first-order viscous hydrodynamics, and report the results of a complete mode census. The
one-dimensional frequency-domain solvers cover radial and non-radial Cowling $f/p$ towers,
buoyancy ($g$) modes of stratified stars, interface ($i$) modes at a first-order phase
transition, full-GR polar quasi-normal modes with gravitational-wave damping, axial viscous
$w$-modes, tidal deformability, moment of inertia, and $r$-modes, across seven equations of
state. The viscous sector is recomputed in the three \BDNK\ hydrodynamic frames of
Clarisse~\emph{et al.}: the repository reproduces their published characteristic speeds
$c_+ = 1.00/0.85/0.74$ to $10^{-16}$, $3\times10^{-4}$ and $2\times10^{-3}$ from $(a_1,a_2)$
alone; hydrodynamic observables are frame-invariant to round-off in flat space and to $0.34\%$
for the stellar axial $w$-mode, while the non-hydrodynamic gaps move by exactly the frame ratios
$4.00\times$ and $7.00\times$. A structural result is that $a_1$ (the energy-frame time
$\tau_\varepsilon$) is inert in the axial sector and, empirically, in the 3+1D polar sector as
well. Searching for EOS-independent combinations of modes, every two-mode ratio fails across a
mass sequence; the $\sqrt{\ell/2}$ scaling of the $f$-mode $\ell$-ladder is shown to be a
fixed-mass artifact and the ladder relation an algebraic identity, while a four-observable
pure-spectral relation among $(f_f,\tau_f,f_w,\tau_w)$ survives at $0.70\%$ leave-one-EOS-out
scatter and a new $w$-Love relation at $0.38\%$. The relativistic Cowling approximation is shown
to break the $f$-Love relation ($1.81\%$ vs $0.18\%$). Finally the three 3+1D Cartesian engines
are validated against the 1D solvers: the linear Cowling engine reproduces the $f$-mode to
$<0.1\%$ for smooth barotropes but excites a growing surface mode for crusted EOS; the \BDNK\
engine gives a frame-invariant viscous damping rate converging at third order to $96$--$97\%$
of the Navier--Stokes dissipation integral and shows the linear stability bound
$\tau_Q w_0 c_s^2 > \tfrac43\eta$ to be sufficient but not necessary; the nonlinear DG engine
represents a non-axisymmetric $m=2$ perturbation and bounds the grid's violation of the
$m$-degeneracy at $10^{-4}$. All three 3D engines freeze the metric, so gravitational-wave
damping, tidal deformability and the $w$-modes are structurally out of their reach. We keep
every retracted claim and every engine defect in the record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and conventions}
%======================================================================

Throughout, $G=c=1$. Two geometric length conventions coexist in the code and were the source
of one bug found in this work: \texttt{ShumPolytrope(100.0)} (the $\Gamma=2$, $K=100$
polytrope) is \emph{solar-mass}-geometric, so that its central density
$\varepsilon_c = 0.00128 + 100\cdot0.00128^2$ gives the standard benchmark star
$M = 1.4002\,\Msun$, $R = 14.154$~km; the piecewise polytropes and \texttt{PolytropeEnergy}
are \emph{kilometre}-geometric. Frequencies are converted with $\mathrm{kHz\to km}$ and the
appropriate unit length $L_{\rm unit}$; omitting the $L_{\rm unit}$ argument silently lowers
km-geometric frequencies by $1.4766$ (this exact bug exists at
\texttt{test/test\_universal\_relations.jl:80}). Compactness is $C = 1.476625\,M/R$ with $M$ in
$\Msun$ and $R$ in km; every data file produced here satisfies this to $<10^{-5}$.

Three verification conventions governed the whole study and are stated once here:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A quantity flat at a single mass is not universal; universality is tested along a mass
sequence and across EOS simultaneously.
\item Identical digits across a swept parameter mean the parameter is not being forwarded.
This failure mode occurred three separate times (an unforwarded $\hat\tau$, a defaulted-off
junction switch, and a Julia parse of \texttt{2f1} as the \texttt{Float32} literal $20.0$).
\item A time-domain frequency from a record of length $T$ has spectral resolution
$\Delta f = 1/T$; any claimed effect smaller than $\Delta f$ rests on parametric peak
estimation and is quoted as such, never as a resolved line.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background stars}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff integration}
The static background is
\begin{align}
m' &= 4\pi r^2 \varepsilon, \qquad
\nu' = \frac{2(m + 4\pi r^3 p)}{r(r-2m)}, \qquad
p' = -\frac{(\varepsilon+p)(m + 4\pi r^3 p)}{r(r-2m)},
\end{align}
integrated outward by classical RK4 from a Taylor-seeded regular centre,
$m(h) = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\varepsilon_c h^3$, $p(h) = p_c - 2\pi(\varepsilon_c+p_c)(\varepsilon_c/3+p_c)h^2$.
The surface is located by linear interpolation of the last step at $p\to0$, and $\nu$ is shifted
so that $e^{\nu(R)} = 1-2M/R$. The metric is $ds^2 = -e^{\nu}dt^2 + e^{\lambda}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$
with $\nu,\lambda$ the \emph{full} exponents, so $\Phi' = \nu'/2$ is the local gravity and
$\alpha = e^{\nu/2}$ the lapse. The step is $h = 2\times10^{-4}$ (production) or
$5\times10^{-5}$ (radial modes); the interface-mode TOV steps \emph{through} a density jump,
which caps its convergence at first order in $h$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:imode}).

\subsection{Equations of state}
\label{sec:eos}
Nine EOS families were exercised (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}):
\begin{description}
\item[Shum polytrope] $p = K\rho^2$, $\varepsilon = \rho + p$, $K=100$ ($\Msun$-geometric); the
Font--Stergioulas--Kokkotas benchmark star.
\item[\texttt{PolytropeEnergy}] $p = \kappa\varepsilon^{1+1/n}$; used at $\Gamma = 2, 2.5, 3$ as
outlier controls, and at $(\kappa,n) = (100,1)$ as the Bussi\`eres EOS1 axial benchmark star.
\item[Read \emph{et al.} piecewise polytropes] SLy, APR4, H4, MS1 from Table~III of
Ref.~\cite{Read2009}: a fixed four-piece SLy crust joined to three high-density segments
$p = K_i\rho^{\Gamma_i}$ at $\rho_1 = 10^{14.7}$, $\rho_2 = 10^{15}$~g\,cm$^{-3}$, with
$\varepsilon_i = (1+a_i)\rho + K_i\rho^{\Gamma_i}/(\Gamma_i-1)$ and $a_i$ fixed by continuity.
The curve is tabulated into a monotone cubic-Hermite \texttt{TabulatedBarotrope}. The
\textbf{package default of $N=2000$ table nodes is unsafe}: it puts a silent $6\%$ error in
SLy's radial fundamental ($4.584$ vs $4.328$~kHz at $N=8000$). All production runs used
$N=8000$. Backgrounds reproduce the published $M_{\max}$ and $R_{1.4}$ to $0.03$--$0.9\%$.
\item[Tabulated Shum] \texttt{tabulate(ShumPolytrope,\,1e-11,\,1e-1,\,4000)}, exercising the
interpolation path; agrees with the analytic Shum $f/p$ tower to nine significant figures.
The lower table bound is load-bearing: $e_{\rm lo} = 10^{-10}$ returns a \emph{spurious
fundamental} below the true one.
\item[Stratified ideal gas] \texttt{solve\_tov\_idealgas($\Gamma$, $\Gamma_{\rm struct}$)}:
hydrostatic structure follows index $\Gamma_{\rm struct}$ while perturbations are adiabatic with
index $\Gamma$; $\Gamma \neq \Gamma_{\rm struct}$ gives $N^2 \neq 0$. The only family supporting
$g$-modes.
\item[Phase-transition hybrid] $\varepsilon(p) = \varepsilon_{\rm poly}(p) + \Delta\varepsilon\,
\Theta(p - p_t)$ on the $\Gamma=2$ base: a genuine density discontinuity at constant pressure,
which the continuous piecewise polytropes cannot represent. Lives in the shooting script, not
yet in \texttt{src/eos/}.
\end{description}

\section{One-dimensional perturbation solvers}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Radial Cowling modes}
The Lagrangian displacement obeys $A_2\xi'' + A_1\xi' + A_0\xi = \omega^2\xi$ with
$A_{0,1,2}$ explicit in $(m,p,\varepsilon,\nu,\cs,\cs')$; second-order central differences on a
uniform grid, $\xi(0)=0$, $\Delta p(R)=0$, solved as a matrix eigenproblem. The operator needs
$d^2p/d\varepsilon^2$: \textbf{no \texttt{d2pde2} method exists for \texttt{TabulatedBarotrope}},
so the radial solver dies with a \texttt{MethodError} on every tabulated EOS (a blocker still in
\texttt{src/}). The atmosphere floor $\varepsilon_f = 10^{-9}\varepsilon_c$ is hardcoded and
evaluates $\cs$ there, producing \texttt{Inf}/\texttt{NaN} or a spurious mode depending on the
table bounds.

\subsection{Non-radial Cowling $f/p$ modes}
\label{sec:fp}
The McDermott--Van~Horn--Scholl / Sotani $(W,V)$ system~\cite{Sotani2024},
\begin{align}
\frac{dW}{dr} &= \frac{1}{\cs^2}\Big[\omega^2 r^2\frac{e^{\lambda}}{e^{\nu}}V + \frac{\nu'}{2}W\Big]
 - \ell(\ell+1)e^{\lambda}V, \qquad
\frac{dV}{dr} = \nu' V - \frac{e^{\lambda}}{r^2}W,
\end{align}
with $W\sim r^{\ell+1}$, $V\sim -r^{\ell}/\ell$ at the centre and $\Delta p = 0$ at the surface,
$\omega^2R^2(e^\lambda/e^\nu)V(R) + \tfrac{\nu'}{2}W(R) = 0$. RK4 shooting from the centre and
Brent root-finding in $\omega^2$ on the surface mismatch, $N=2500$--$4000$ steps. A hardcoded
surface cut at $R(1-10^{-3})$ contributes $+0.015/+0.032/+0.055\%$ to $p_2/p_3/p_4$; $f$ and
$p_1$ are converged to $<0.01\%$.

\subsection{Gravity modes}
The stratified $(U,V)$ form~\cite{Jaikumar2021,GaertigKokkotas2009}, $U = r^2e^{\lambda/2}\xi_r$,
$V = \delta P/(\varepsilon+P)$,
\begin{align}
\frac{dU}{dr} &= \frac{g}{\cs^2}U + e^{\lambda/2}\Big[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)e^{\nu}}{\omega^2} - \frac{r^2}{\cs^2}\Big]V,\qquad
\frac{dV}{dr} = e^{\lambda/2-\nu}\frac{\omega^2 - N^2}{r^2}U + g\,\Delta(c^{-2})V,
\end{align}
with $g = \nu'/2$, $\Delta(c^{-2}) = 1/c_e^2 - 1/\cs^2$ and the relativistic Brunt--V\"ais\"al\"a
frequency $N^2 = g^2\Delta(c^{-2})e^{\nu-\lambda}$. $\Gamma > \Gamma_{\rm struct} \Rightarrow
N^2 > 0 \Rightarrow$ a real $g$-tower below the $f$-mode; $\Gamma < \Gamma_{\rm struct}$ gives
$N^2<0$ and imaginary frequencies (convective instability), both verified. Two defects: the
mode classifier at \texttt{GravityModes.jl:324} uses \texttt{argmin(nodes)} with no tie-break and
misidentifies the $f$-mode when two zero-node modes appear; and the default scan floor
$\omega^2_{\rm lo} = 10^{-4}$ sits \emph{above} the entire $g$-tower for stratification
$\delta \lesssim 0.02$, silently returning zero $g$-modes on a star that has them.

\subsection{Interface modes at a first-order phase transition}
\label{sec:imode}
Written for this work. With $Y \equiv \delta p$ and $Rh$ related to the displacement by
$\xi_r = \alpha\,Rh/(\omega w_0 e^{\lambda})$, the shooting system is
\begin{align}
\frac{dY}{dr} &= \frac{\omega Rh}{\alpha} - \Phi'\frac{1+\cs^2}{\cs^2}Y, \\
\frac{dRh}{dr} &= -\frac{\omega e^{\lambda}}{\cs^2\alpha}Y + \frac{\ell(\ell+1)\alpha e^{\lambda}}{\omega r^2}Y
 - \frac{d\ln g}{dr}Rh, \qquad g \equiv \frac{r^2\alpha\rho_0}{w_0\sqrt{e^{\lambda}}},
\end{align}
with the free-surface discriminant $D = Y + \xi_r p_0'$ normalised by $|Y|+|\xi_r p_0'|$. The
\emph{structural} fact that makes the conversion-timescale question a clean null test: since
$d\ln g/dr \ni -d\ln w_0/dr$, marching straight through the discontinuity integrates the delta
function to $Rh_{\rm out}/Rh_{\rm in} = w_{\rm out}/w_{\rm in}$ exactly, i.e.\ it enforces
$\xi_r$ continuity by itself, while $Y$ stays continuous because its coefficients are bounded.
That is \emph{rapid} conversion. \emph{Slow} conversion (Lagrangian $\Delta p$ continuous rather
than $\delta p$) adds exactly one term at the junction,
\begin{equation}
Y \;\to\; Y + \xi_r\,\Phi'\,(w_{\rm out}-w_{\rm in}),
\end{equation}
and the $i$-mode exists if and only if that term is present. The claim was challenged by a
verifier as ``wrong in sign'' and settled numerically: integrating $Rh$ across the resolved
interface and dividing by a smooth no-jump control gives $0.824804\to0.819238\to0.816287$ as the
half-width halves, first order, extrapolating to $0.8133$ against the prediction $0.81342$
($0.01\%$); the opposite sign would give $1.2294$. The junction skip width $\epsilon$ and the
background step $h$ are both first-order error sources ($\Delta f_i \propto \epsilon$ exactly,
as expected from dropping a shell of thickness $2\epsilon$), extrapolating to
$f_i(\ell=2,\Delta\varepsilon=2\times10^{-4}) = 0.93424\kHz$ at $\sim0.01\%$. Two bugs were
found and are recorded because both faked a \emph{wrong-but-plausible} answer: the junction
branch was keyed to a caller-supplied kwarg that defaulted off (every early ``$i$-mode'' was a
plain star, giving $59$--$178$~Hz, $10\times$ below the published band), and $w_{\rm in/out}$
were read by linear interpolation of the background, which smears the jump over one cell.
Stars beyond the Seidov limit $\Delta\varepsilon_S = \varepsilon_{\rm out}/2 + 3p_t/2$ are
radially unstable and are flagged.

\subsection{Full-GR polar quasi-normal modes}
The Lindblom--Detweiler even-parity system~\cite{DetweilerLindblom1985} in the numerically stable
variables $\{H_1,K,W,X\}$ as written by Kr\"uger, Gaertig \& Kokkotas~\cite{KGK2011}: two
centre-regular solutions combined to satisfy $X(R)=0$; at the surface $(H_1,K)$ is mapped to the
Zerilli function, integrated in vacuum to a matching radius $a$; the purely-outgoing condition is
imposed analytically via the Chandrasekhar--Detweiler transform of the Regge--Wheeler Leaver
continued fraction (isospectrality); the QNM condition is the vanishing Wronskian
$Z_{\rm in}Z'_{\rm up} - Z_{\rm up}Z'_{\rm in} = 0$, root-found by a complex secant. Frequencies
are insensitive to $a$ ($<2\times10^{-6}$) but $\tau_f$ moves by $33$--$35\%$ between
$a/R = 3$ and $30$ and the method branch-jumps to the $w$-mode by $a/R=50$; production uses
$a/R=12$ and quotes $\tau$ only with $a$ stated.

\subsection{Axial viscous $w$-modes}
A faithful port of Bussi\`eres \emph{et al.}~\cite{Bussieres2026}: coupled axial wave equations
for the metric $\psi$ and the fluid $Z$ with the Regge--Wheeler potential and \BDNK\ couplings,
two-seed RK4 interior shooting with the surface regularity condition, exterior vacuum RW
integration, Leaver ``up'' continued fraction, and $\Delta(\omega) = \psi'_{\rm in}/\psi_{\rm in}
- \psi'_{\rm up}/\psi_{\rm up} = 0$. Viscosity parametrizations A: $\eta = \hat\eta(\rho+p)L_0\cs^2$,
$\tau = \hat\tau L_0\hat\eta$; B: $\eta = \hat\eta pL_0$, $\tau = \hat\tau L_0 p/\rho$.
Reproduces Table~II~A1 of Ref.~\cite{Bussieres2026} to $+0.0002\%$ in $f$ and $-0.035\%$ in
$\tau$; a single solve takes $0.05$--$1$~s. A structural observation (Sec.~\ref{sec:frames}):
the solver assigns the energy-frame coefficient $\theta$ at line 291 and never reads it.

\subsection{Tidal deformability, moment of inertia, $r$-modes}
$k_2$ from the Riccati equation $ry' + y^2 + yF + r^2Q = 0$, $y(0)=2$, with the
Hinderer closed form~\cite{Hinderer2008,Postnikov2010} and $\Lambda = \tfrac23k_2C^{-5}$. The moment
of inertia from Hartle's frame-drag equation $\frac{d}{dr}[r^4 j\varpi'] + 4r^3j'\varpi = 0$,
$j = e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}$~\cite{Hartle1967}, with $I = J/\Omega$ and $\bar I = I/M^3$.
$r$-modes from Lindblom--Owen--Morsink~\cite{LOM1998}: $\sigma = \tfrac23\Omega$,
$\omega = -\tfrac43\Omega$, with $1/\tau = 1/\tau_{\rm GW} + 1/\tau_{sv} + 1/\tau_{bv}$; the
Newtonian $n=1$ Lane--Emden integral $\tilde J = 1.635270\times10^{-2}$ reproduces LOM98's
$1.635\times10^{-2}$ ($+0.02\%$). One calibrated constant absorbs the O(1) $\delta\rho$
eigenfunction normalisation of the bulk-viscosity integral.

\section{BDNK causal viscous hydrodynamics}
\label{sec:bdnk}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Constitutive relations and hydrodynamic frames}
The general-frame stress tensor~\cite{BDN2022}
\begin{align}
T^{\mu\nu} &= (\varepsilon + \mathcal A)u^\mu u^\nu + (P + \Pi)\Delta^{\mu\nu} - 2\eta\sigma^{\mu\nu}
 + u^\mu\mathcal Q^\nu + u^\nu\mathcal Q^\mu,\\
\mathcal A &= \tau_\varepsilon[D\varepsilon + (\varepsilon+P)\theta],\qquad
\Pi = -\zeta\theta + \tau_P[D\varepsilon + (\varepsilon+P)\theta],\\
\mathcal Q^\nu &= \tau_Q[(\varepsilon+P)Du^\nu + \cs^2\Delta^{\nu\lambda}\partial_\lambda\varepsilon],
\end{align}
with $D = u\cdot\nabla$, $\theta = \nabla\cdot u$. The relaxation times
$(\tau_\varepsilon,\tau_P,\tau_Q)$ are the frame; physical predictions must not depend on them
at first order in gradients, while the non-hydrodynamic (gapped) modes do.

\subsection{The Clarisse frames and their non-conformal generalisation}
Clarisse \emph{et al.}~\cite{Clarisse2025} parametrize $\tau_\varepsilon = a_1(1/T)(\eta/s)$,
$\tau_Q = a_2(1/T)(\eta/s)$ under $a_1\ge4$, $a_2 \ge 3a_1/(a_1-1)$, and use
(Table~\ref{tab:frames}) $F_1 = (25/4,25/7)$, $F_2 = (25/2,25/3)$, $F_3 = (25,25)$. $F_1$
\emph{saturates} the second bound exactly, $3(25/4)/(21/4) = 25/7$, which is why its $c_+$ is
exactly~1: it is the marginally causal frame, and any stellar result in which $F_1$ looks cleaner
than $F_2,F_3$ should be treated as suspect. At zero chemical potential $w = \varepsilon+P = sT$,
so $(1/T)(\eta/s) = \eta/w$ and the stellar map is
\begin{equation}
\tau_\varepsilon = a_1\,\eta/w_0(r), \qquad \tau_Q = a_2\,\eta/w_0(r),
\label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
position-dependent inside a star. Under this map the repository's own linear stability bound
for the polar sound sector, $\tau_Qw_0\cs^2 > \tfrac43\eta$, reduces to the $\eta$-independent
condition $a_2\cs^2 > 4/3$.

\subsection{Flat-space dispersion and characteristic speeds}
The sound-channel quartic of Kovtun~\cite{Kovtun2019} in the general frame
$(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\pi_1,\pi_2,\theta)$ is solved by companion-matrix roots; the
Clarisse frames map as $\theta = \tau_Qw$, $\varepsilon_1 = \tau_\varepsilon w/\cs^2$,
$\varepsilon_2 = \pi_1 = \cs^2\varepsilon_1$, $\pi_2 = \cs^4\varepsilon_1 - \zeta$ (the last being
Kovtun's footnote-8 compensation that pins the physical bulk viscosity across frames). The
large-$k$ biquadratic (Kovtun Eq.~4.18) gives the characteristic speeds. The separate
Keeble--Redondo-Yuste biquadratic in \texttt{Causality.jl} is a different characteristic system
(it lacks the $\pi_1,\varepsilon_2$ cross terms) and does \emph{not} reproduce the Clarisse table
under any feeding; it is retained for cross-reference only.

\subsection{Polar \BDNK\ operator (frequency domain)}
The linearised polar \BDNK--Cowling system reduced to one radial dimension for fixed $\ell$,
five fields $(\hat\rho, \delta\hat S_r, \delta\hat S_\theta, \hat u^r, \hat u^\theta)$, plus a sixth
(the Lagrangian displacement $\xi$) when stratified; QNMs are the eigenvalues of the resulting
matrix, tracked by complex continuation in $\hat\eta$. Transport channels: relaxation
$\tau_R = \hat\tau + c_\tau\hat\eta$ (recovery denominator $\tau_Rw_0-\eta$), reactive conduction
$\kappa_Q$, and momentum shear $\nu_{\rm mom}$ (the genuine damper). A corrected free-surface
boundary condition ($\Delta p = 0 \Rightarrow \partial_t\delta\rho = -\delta v^r p_0'/(\cs^2h_0)$)
moved the $f$-mode from $+8.91\%$ to $+0.18\%$ of the shooting value. A verifier found that the
velocity recovery omits the lapse multiplying the whole bracket, so the faithful relaxation slot
is $\tau_R = \tau_Q/\alpha$; the polar \emph{damping} rates from this operator were withheld on
that basis (Sec.~\ref{sec:frames}).

%======================================================================
\section{The 3+1D Cartesian engines}
\label{sec:engines}
%======================================================================

All three engines freeze the metric (Cowling). This is a structural limit, not a resolution one:
there is no gravitational radiation and no metric perturbation, so $w$-modes, GW damping times,
$\Lambda$, and hence $f$-Love and $w$-Love cannot exist in them; neither can $g$, $i$, or $r$
modes (no stratification, no discontinuity, no rotation).

\subsection{\texttt{CowlingEvolve3D}: linearized, cut-cell surface}
Evolves $(\delta\varepsilon, \delta S_i)$ with $\delta S_i = (\varepsilon_0+p_0)\delta v_i$ so that
$(\varepsilon_0+p_0)$ cancels from every RHS and the $1/w_0$ surface blow-up disappears:
\begin{align}
\partial_t\delta\varepsilon &= -\frac{1}{\sqrt\gamma}\partial_i[\alpha\sqrt\gamma\,\delta S^i] - \alpha\Phi'(n_i\delta S^i),\qquad
\partial_t\delta S_i = -\alpha[\partial_i\delta p + \Phi' n_i(\delta p + \delta\varepsilon)],
\end{align}
$\gamma^{ij} = \delta_{ij} + (e^{-2\lambda}-1)n_in_j$, second-order central differences,
Kreiss--Oliger dissipation $\sigma_{\rm KO}$, RK4. The surface is treated by cut cells: both the
volume fraction $\kappa$ and the six face apertures reduce to one-dimensional quadratures of the
same disc--rectangle area, verified against the analytic sphere to $10^{-7}$ in volume and
$4\pi R^2$ at second order in closure. Seeds and diagnostics are $\ell=2$, $m=0$ only
($2Z^2-X^2-Y^2$). \textbf{The default \texttt{cut=false} is wrong by $-6.95\%$} and does not
converge away between $N=32$ and $40$, from a run with no visible symptom.

\subsection{\texttt{CowlingBDNK3D}: full-frame causal viscous}
Linearised \BDNK\ with the frame times kept, so the dissipative corrections enter the conserved
densities and the system is hyperbolic. The primitives are recovered from
\begin{align}
\delta E &= \delta\varepsilon + \tau_\varepsilon\big(\tfrac1\alpha\partial_t\delta\varepsilon + w_0\theta\big),\qquad
\delta S^i = w_0\delta v^i + (\tau_Qw_0-\eta)\tfrac1\alpha\partial_t\delta v^i + \tau_Q\cs^2\gamma^{il}\partial_l\delta\varepsilon,
\end{align}
with the covariant expansion $\theta = \partial_i\delta v^i + (\Phi' + A'/A)(n\cdot\delta v)$ and
the closure $\tau_P = \cs^2\tau_\varepsilon$ forced by $\Pi = \cs^2\mathcal A$ (using
$\tau_P = \tau_\varepsilon$ reopens a documented primitive-feedback instability). The recovery
denominator $\tau_Qw_0 - \eta$ is the causality margin. The viscous damping is measured by the
\emph{difference protocol}: $\gamma_{\rm visc} = \gamma(\hat\eta) - \gamma(0)$ at identical grid and
frame, which cancels the numerical floor; at $N=40$ that floor is still $52\%$ of
$\gamma_{\rm visc}$, so the protocol is mandatory.

\subsection{\texttt{DGCart3D}: nonlinear nodal RKDG}
Full 3D tensor-product nodal Runge--Kutta discontinuous Galerkin Valencia GRHydro on the frozen
metric: densitized conserved set $D = \sqrt\gamma\rho W$, $S_i = \sqrt\gamma\rho hW^2v_i$,
$\tau = \sqrt\gamma(\rho hW^2 - p - \rho W)$, directional Rusanov sweeps, SSP-RK3, a per-element
TVB troubled-cell / Zhang--Shu positivity limiter, an atmosphere, and the well-balanced
lake-at-rest device (the static background's DG RHS is stored and subtracted). Octant grid with
reflecting symmetry and a full 3-momentum, so $\mathrm{Re}\,Y_{22}\propto n_x^2-n_y^2$ can be
represented; $\texttt{dgcart3d\_quadrupole\_m2}$ is the $m\neq0$ detector. Two facts govern
everything it produced: the octant's three \emph{separate} reflections admit only the $E_g$
doublet $\{Y_{20},\mathrm{Re}\,Y_{22}\}$ of the $\ell=2$ quintet, so the $T_{2g}$ triplet (all of
$m=1$) is structurally absent; and \textbf{its background is not static} --- the well-balanced
subtraction is exact but \texttt{\_limit!} is not, so $\rho_c$ erodes $-10\%$ by $t = 600\,\Msun$
(4.6 $f$-periods) and $-31$ to $-43\%$ by $t=3000$.

\subsection{Frequency extraction}
\label{sec:extract}
Time series are analysed by a parabolic-vertex-refined periodogram and an independent
matrix-pencil (SVD, 14--16 poles) estimator that must agree; every run is gated on finiteness and
on the end/start envelope ratio before any transform, and a record whose clean window is below
$10$ periods is voided. At affordable resolution the usable record is $23$ periods
($\Delta f/f = 4.4\%$) for the linear and \BDNK\ engines and $4.6$ periods ($\Delta f/f = 21.6\%$)
for the DG engine --- capped not by cost but by the background erosion above. \emph{Every
sub-percent 3D figure in this paper is a parametric estimate, not a resolved line.}

Three results stand out from Table~\ref{tab:robust}.

\emph{The control is real.} A deliberate $40\times$ frame perturbation moves both flat-space gaps
by exactly $40.00\times$ while $v_s$ and $\Gamma$ stay flat to $1.2\times10^{-12}\%$; an
$a_2$-only $112\times$ sweep moves only the heat-flux gap. A frame-independence claim without
such a control is void.

\emph{$a_1$ is inert in the axial sector.} $\mathcal A = \tau_\varepsilon[u\cdot\nabla\varepsilon
+ w\nabla\cdot u]$ is a scalar; odd-parity perturbations have $\delta\varepsilon = \delta P = 0$
and $\nabla\cdot\delta u = 0$, so $\mathcal A\equiv0$ and $\tau_\varepsilon$ never enters. The
code agrees. So $F_1/F_2/F_3$ are three values of $\tau_Q$ and nothing else there, and the
$0.34\%$ residue is not an offset: it scales as $\eta^{1.99}$ while the physical viscous shift
($-4.06\%$ from the inviscid $10.501355$~kHz) scales as $\eta^{0.98}$ --- one full order higher
in the dissipative expansion.

\emph{The polar test block is refuted.} \texttt{test\_frame\_independence.jl} block~(c) recorded a
``fixed $+6$--$7\%$ $f$-mode offset present at all frames''. That reproduces exactly --- under
the \emph{old} no-slip boundary condition. With the corrected free surface it collapses to
$+0.10$--$0.22\%$. The offset was never a solver property. The polar damping rates from this
operator are withheld: with the recovery lapse restored the $f$-mode damping frame-spread changes
by $\sim20\times$ and the $p_1$ damping spread ($18/25/30\%$ at $N_r = 100/140/180$) is unresolved.

\section{Results III: EOS-independent combinations of modes}
\label{sec:universal}
%======================================================================

Tested on mass sequences: 7 EOS, 88 stars, $M = 0.55$--$2.62\,\Msun$, $C = 0.073$--$0.318$, all
mode families on one background grid. The pipeline gate: I-Love against the \emph{published}
Yagi--Yunes coefficients~\cite{YagiYunes2013} with nothing fitted gives $-0.171\%$ mean,
$0.293\%$ std, $1.118\%$ max --- literature grade.

\emph{Every two-mode ratio fails} (Table~\ref{tab:ratios}). The $\sqrt{\ell/2}$ result deserves its
own paragraph because it is the canonical trap: $\omega_f(\ell)/\omega_f(2) = 1.2216, 1.4072$ at
$M=1.4$ across the four realistic EOS (spreads $0.40\%$, $0.63\%$) sits within $0.5\%$ of
$\sqrt{3/2}, \sqrt{2}$ --- and drifts \emph{monotonically with compactness}, moving $2$--$4\times$
more along one EOS's mass sequence than across all EOS at fixed mass, crossing $\sqrt{\ell/2}$ at
nearly the same compactness $C = 0.186$--$0.205$ for every EOS. $M = 1.4\,\Msun$ merely lands near
that crossing. Likewise the $\ell$-ladder relation $f_4/f_2 = 1.0127\,(f_3/f_2)^{1.643}$, with a
spectacular $0.039\%$ leave-one-EOS-out scatter, is an algebraic identity: if
$\omega_\ell\propto\ell^b$ then $\ln r_4/\ln r_3 = \ln2/\ln1.5 = 1.7095$ identically, the fit is a
reparametrisation of the single number $b$ (which ranges $0.44$--$0.53$ and drifts with $C$),
and $b\approx0.49$ is the known surface-wave asymptotic.

$\varepsilon\propto(R-r)^n$: $0.50/0.67/1.005$ (stable) versus $1.84/1.87/1.95/1.95$ (unstable),
monotone over 19 decades of end/start ratio. The atmosphere-sound-speed hypothesis was tested
(all $77\,040$ atmosphere cells zeroed, SLy $N=48$) and refuted ($+0.596$ vs $+0.595$
e-folds/period). No affordable configuration gives a converging crusted-EOS $f$-mode; the
mechanism is open.

%======================================================================
\section{Retractions and defects}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Claims withdrawn during verification}
\begin{itemize}
\item Radial spectra of the piecewise polytropes (APR4 fundamental $6.6\%$ off; H4 $2.0\%$);
root cause the Hermite-table second derivative.
\item All $r$-mode instability windows: two of five stars were the wrong mass ($0.95$ and
$1.93\,\Msun$ labelled $1.4$) and three more mislabelled. The algebraic result and the
Lane--Emden $\tilde J$ stand.
\item Polar \BDNK\ damping frame-spreads (missing lapse).
\item Viscous axial numbers for isentropic $\Gamma=5/3$ and the phase-transition EOS at
$\eta_c=10^{31}$ (ladders never reached it).
\item ``$g_1/g_2$ constant to $0.1\%$ over $64\times$ in $\delta$'' --- true only for $\delta\le0.05$.
\item Universal-relation claims about the Cowling $f$-mode's deviation from AK98 being
monotone in compactness; the true Cowling overestimate ($+24.8$--$+29.4\%$ at $M=1.4$; $+13.9$ to
$+32.9\%$ over the full 88-star set) is far larger than the $+5.9$--$17.2\%$ inferred from AK
residuals.
\item ``The pure-spectral route is $2.5\times$ more EOS-independent than $(M,R)$'' --- comparable,
not superior, at matched parameter count.
\item The $\ell$-ladder relation as a universal relation (algebraic identity); the $\sqrt{\ell/2}$
scaling as anything but a fixed-mass artifact; the $i$-mode's $0.896$ factor as a universal
constant.
\item Early $i$-mode frequencies of $59$--$178$~Hz (defaulted-off junction).
\item ``Converged to $0.2\%$'' and ``$f$-mode damping agrees to $3.6\%$'' from the spherical
\BDNK\ engine (a crossing, documented in \texttt{test\_sph\_shear\_qnm.jl}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Defects still present in \texttt{src/}}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{d2pde2} has no \texttt{TabulatedBarotrope} method: the radial solver is blocked on
every tabulated EOS.
\item \texttt{piecewise\_polytrope} ships with $N=2000$ table nodes: a silent $6\%$ error.
\item \texttt{RadialModes} atmosphere floor: \texttt{Inf}/\texttt{NaN} or a spurious fundamental
depending on table bounds.
\item \texttt{GravityModes.jl:324} classifier tie-break; \texttt{:276} scan floor above the
$g$-tower for $\delta\lesssim0.02$.
\item \texttt{test\_universal\_relations.jl:80} omits \texttt{Lunit\_km=1.0}.
\item Polar operator velocity recovery omits the lapse ($\tau_R = \tau_Q/\alpha$); distinct from
the previously fixed lapse bug in \texttt{CowlingBDNK3D}.
\item \texttt{setup\_evo3d} default \texttt{cut=false}: $-6.95\%$ on $f$ with no symptom.
\item $\sigma_{\rm KO}$ is an uncontrolled systematic ($\pm0.25\%$ cut-cell, $>7$~pp masked).
\item \texttt{Background3D.build\_star3d:116} sets $\rho_0 = \varepsilon - p$, exact only for
$\Gamma=2$; harmless to the linear engines, fatal to any nonlinear user.
\item \texttt{DGCart3D} background erodes $-10\%$ in 4.6 periods (\texttt{\_limit!} is not
well-balanced).
\item \texttt{test\_frame\_independence.jl} block (c) asserts an offset that was the old BC.
\item Julia: \texttt{2f1} is the \texttt{Float32} literal $20.0$.
\end{enumerate}

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
%======================================================================

The toolkit's one-dimensional solvers are validated against the published benchmarks they
target to $0.02$--$0.05\%$ ($f/p$ tower), $0.0002\%$ (axial viscous), and literature grade
(I-Love), and the \BDNK\ frame machinery reproduces the Clarisse characteristic speeds from
$(a_1,a_2)$ alone. On that foundation: hydrodynamic observables are frame-invariant with a live
control; $a_1$ is inert wherever the perturbation carries no scalar content; the relativistic
Cowling approximation breaks $f$-Love and must not be used in an inversion; no two-mode ratio is
EOS-independent, but a four-observable pure-spectral relation and a $w$-Love relation are; and
the interface mode at a first-order phase transition is established with a structural rather than
fitted null test. The three 3+1D engines are validated where they can be --- the linear Cowling
$f$-mode to $<0.1\%$ on smooth barotropes, the \BDNK\ viscous damping rate at third-order
convergence --- and their limits are recorded where they cannot: the metric is frozen, crusted
surfaces destabilise the linear engine, the DG background erodes, and time-domain resolution at
affordable cost is $4$--$22\%$ of $f$. The single number to fix first is
\texttt{setup\_evo3d}'s default.
